Component 01 · Artifact collection

Artifacts.

Eight of the thirteen assignments I submitted this semester, chosen for variety of type — text, image, video, peer response, revision. Each has an image of the artifact, a screenshot of the submission, and a short note on what it is and why it's here.

01VideoWeek 7 · Project 1 · ~6 min

Digital Literacy Narrative — final video

What it is. Project 1: a six-minute video about my history with computers, edited in CapCut. Three scenes — a third-grade computer-lab story, fifth-grade GPT-2, and what I do with AI in SF now.

Why it's here. It's the biggest piece of work I made this semester. It also forced me to actually edit video for school, which I'd never done.

Achievement & struggle. Finishing the cut. The middle section is too talky; I'd cut about 40 seconds if I had another pass at it.

Lesson. Writing for video is not the same as writing for the page. Stuff I would have left alone in an essay didn't survive being read aloud.

02Research essay + videoWeek 13 · Project 2

Conspiracy Theory Rhetorical Analysis — MKUltra in the Bay Area

What it is. Project 2: a rhetorical analysis of conspiracy writing about MKUltra's safehouses in San Francisco. I made both a written version and a short video version.

Why it's here. It's the longest piece of writing I did all semester, and the one with the most sources. The instructor flagged the distinction I make between confirmed and speculative material as the thing that worked.

Achievement & struggle. Getting the final draft to actually argue something specific. The first draft was a slippery-slope sketch I had to mostly redo.

Lesson. Citing a lot of real documents doesn't automatically make an argument true. The connection between the citation and the conclusion is its own thing.

03Personal journalWeek 1 · January 2026

Journal 1

What it is. The first weekly journal of the semester, about teaching myself some Rust over winter break and reading a JEPA paper.

04Peer responseWeek 5 · February 2026

Peer review of Petra Helebrantová's DLN draft

What it is. Written peer feedback on Petra's DLN draft. Hers was about Czech-language software, which is pretty different from mine.

Why it's here. Most of the peer-review I did this semester was vague ("looks great"). This one was the first time I gave specific feedback that pointed at choices the writer was actually making.

Achievement & struggle. Saying something useful instead of just polite. Still erring on the kind side, but less.

Lesson. Reading someone else's draft is a good way to notice what you're doing in your own.

05Revision exerciseDLN workshop · February 2026

Memoir revision template

What it is. A worksheet that walked us through four revision passes on the same paragraph — adding context, swapping verbs, cutting filler words, showing instead of telling.

Why it's here. The final version of the paragraph ended up in the DLN narration, so it's a clear before/after.

Achievement & struggle. Actually doing all four passes instead of stopping after the first. The first revision felt "done."

Lesson. A paragraph I think is finished is often three things trying to share a paragraph break.

07Rhetorical analysisWeek 9 · March 2026

Logos analysis — Trump on the USS Cole and Iran

What it is. A short exercise breaking down how a Trump quote about Iran deterrence is built — it sounds like a logical argument but runs on an assumed premise about military presence.

Why it's here. Useful warm-up for the CT project, where I was about to spend a lot of time looking at the same kind of hidden-premise moves.

Achievement & struggle. Actually labeling the unstated premise instead of just saying "this is rhetoric." The struggle was not letting my own opinion of the politics get in the way of the exercise.

Lesson. A lot of arguments hide their work in the premise.
