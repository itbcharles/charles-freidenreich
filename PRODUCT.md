# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, internship coordinators, and professors who google Charles Freidenreich. They arrive with a name and 30 seconds; the job is to confirm he is real, credible, and worth a reply. Secondary: peers and collaborators grabbing his email or LinkedIn.

## Product Purpose

Charles's personal homepage. A fast, plain, credible calling card: who he is, what he is doing now, what he did before, how to reach him. Success is a recruiter reading it in one pass with zero friction and following a link.

## Brand Personality

Bare, fast, matter-of-fact. The site of someone who respects the reader's time. Explicit reference lane: a computer science researcher's personal page (plain text, system fonts, default-ish blue links, one small photo). The absence of styling IS the statement.

## Anti-references

- AI-slop landing pages: gradients, hero sections, cards, animations, glassmorphism. A prior version of this site had AI-generated artifacts the owner scrubbed ("get rid of the ai slop").
- Anything that adds network requests or render-blocking resources for aesthetics (webfonts, CSS frameworks, JS).
- Template-y portfolio sites with skill bars, testimonial carousels, or scroll effects.

## Design Principles

- Speed is the aesthetic: one HTML file, one small image, inline CSS, no JS, no webfonts.
- Bare, not broken: readable measure, real hierarchy, honest defaults executed precisely.
- Every element must justify its bytes.
- Content changes (a new role, a new project) should be a one-line HTML edit.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Semantic HTML, real headings, alt text on the photo, honors prefers-reduced-motion trivially (there is no motion), works with CSS disabled.
